Launcher

All your apps.
Plus your day.

A full-screen launcher with instant search, folders that sort themselves, and widgets beside your apps: the date, what's playing on a spinning record, the weather and your calendar.

Grouped for you

One click sorts every app into Developer Tools, Productivity, Music and more. Rename, regroup, or hide what you never open.

Your day at a glance

Date, music with controls, weather and a month calendar with your next events — right next to your apps.

Open it your way

⌃⌥L, a hot corner, or the menu bar. Uninstall apps and their leftovers from the right-click menu.

Dock

Truly your Dock.

Widgets live right in the bar: Now Playing, an analog clock, the date, weather, battery, CPU and memory, your next meeting, GitHub, a focus timer. Window previews on hover, magnification, folders, badges and presets.

Presets

Morning Tide, Midnight Focus, Ocean Desk, Developer — a whole new Dock in one click.

Window previews

Hover an app to see its windows, even across Spaces. Click one to jump straight to it.

Any edge

Bottom, left or right, auto-hide, and it steps aside for full-screen apps.

Spaces

Save your whole setup — Dock, widgets, layout — and switch between them.

Dynamic Notch

Hello, notch.

Hover the top of your screen and the Island opens: your song, the date, the weather and your timer side by side. No notch on your Mac? AIO draws one.

Live activities

Volume and brightness, charging, AirPods, downloads, mic and camera in use, meeting countdowns.

Shelf

Drop files on the notch, AirDrop them, drag them back out later.

Clipboard

Recent copies one hover away — links, colours, paths and code.

Calendar

Today and tomorrow, with a Join button for Zoom, Meet and Teams.

Music

The whole screen.

Press ⌃⌥P: the album sleeve with the record sliding out, the background moving in the artwork's colours, and lyrics that fill with light as they're sung. Works with Apple Music, Spotify, browsers — anything that plays.

Synced lyrics

Lyrics scroll with the song; click any line to jump there.

Five styles

Sleeve, Vinyl, Cover, full-screen Art and Waves — switch with 1–5.

All keyboard

Space play · ←→ seek · L lyrics · Esc close.

Everything in between.

Clock, calendar, weather, system, notes, to-dos, GitHub — or describe a widget and let AI write it.

GitHub notifications and reviews, AI through Ollama or any OpenAI-compatible server, and your browser tabs.

Privacy

Yours, and only yours.

AIO runs on your Mac. No account, no analytics, no servers of ours.

  • Screen memory is off by default, pauses with ⌘⇧P, skips password fields and never records the apps you block.
  • API keys and tokens stay in files only you can read, and are never included when you export settings.
  • AI suggestions never run a command until you press Enter.
  • Lyrics come from lrclib.net and weather from Open-Meteo — only the song name or your rough location is sent.

Make your Mac yours.

Free. Updates install themselves.

Download for Mac
!
macOS will warn you the first time — that's expected.

AIO isn't notarized by Apple (that needs a paid developer account), so macOS says “Apple could not verify AIO is free of malware” or that it “is damaged and can't be opened”. It's neither: the source is public and every release is signed with the same certificate. To open it once:

  1. Open Terminal, run xattr -cr /Applications/AIO.app, then open AIO again.
  2. Or open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway.

You only do this once — updates keep their permissions.

1Download

Open the .dmg and drag AIO to Applications.

2Open

Allow it once, as above.

3Grant

Settings → Privacy & Permissions → Grant everything.

Does it replace the macOS Dock?

It hides the system Dock while AIO runs and puts it back when you quit. You can turn the AIO Dock off any time.

Which music apps work?

Anything that shows up in macOS's Now Playing: Apple Music, Spotify, YouTube in a browser, VLC and more.

Is it open source?

The source is public so anyone can read and audit it, but AIO is not open source: it's © Rayan Jain, free to use, and not to be redistributed. See the license.
